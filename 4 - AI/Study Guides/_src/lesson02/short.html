Short note: revision sheet

TopicRemember
PromptText instruction or question given to an AI to get a response.
Prompt engineeringCreating clear, effective inputs to guide an AI to the desired output, ideally first try.
Why importantAccuracy · saves time · creativity · guides logical steps · reliability (summarising, coding, analysis).
TRADETask (what; action verb) · Role (who) · Audience (whom) · Details (length, format, tone, constraints) · Example (few-shot).
Elements of a good promptClear instruction · context · specific details · constraints · examples.
5 techniquesZero-shot (no examples) · few-shot (some examples) · chain-of-thought (reason step by step) · role ("act as a teacher") · constraint ("in 100 words").
ApplicationsContent generation, code generation / debugging, Q&A and tutoring, data analysis and summarisation, chatbots and virtual assistants.
Best practicesSimple direct language · enough context · specify output format · avoid ambiguous or overly broad instructions.
LimitationsErrors / outdated info; biased, incomplete or irrelevant output from poor prompts; no guarantee of perfect results.
Example upgrade"Explain photosynthesis." → "Explain photosynthesis in simple terms for a grade 8 student in about 100 words."
4 pillars (AI Kitchen)Prompt = the order · Context = recipe book (RAG, avoids hallucination) · Harness = safety gear and sandboxed tools (Docker, APIs) · Loop = act, observe, evaluate, loop, terminate (e.g. LangGraph).
Exam tipBe ready to (1) rewrite a vague prompt using TRADE and label each part, (2) name the technique from an example, and (3) match the four engineering pillars to their kitchen and AI meanings.
