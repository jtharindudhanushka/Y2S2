2. Background: the swarm idea (not from your course materials)

A swarm is a group of simple agents with no leader that follow simple local rules, yet together solve a hard problem: ants find the shortest path to food, birds flock, fish school. Swarm intelligence turns that behaviour into search and optimisation algorithms. Key features: many simple agents, local interaction, indirect communication, and a result no single agent plans. Like a GA it keeps a population of solutions, so it is a population-based meta-heuristic (Lesson 11); unlike a GA, the agents stay alive and learn from each other instead of being selected, crossed and replaced.

3. Background: ant colony optimisation (ACO)

  • Real ants leave a chemical trail, pheromone, as they walk. Other ants prefer paths with stronger pheromone. On a shorter path ants make round trips faster, so pheromone builds up faster there: positive feedback. Pheromone also evaporates, so old, poor paths fade.
  • Algorithm idea: each ant builds a route (e.g. a travelling-salesman tour) choosing the next step with probability proportional to the pheromone (and usually the shortness of the step); then pheromone evaporates and the ants deposit more on short routes.
  • Choice rule (simple form): P(path) = pheromone on the path / total pheromone. Update: τ ← (1 − ρ) τ + deposits, where ρ is the evaporation rate and a shorter route L deposits more (e.g. Q / L).

Illustrative numbers: two paths: short (L = 2) with pheromone 4, long (L = 4) with pheromone 1. P(short) = 4 / (4 + 1) = 0.8. Say 8 ants walk: about 6 take the short path and 2 the long one. With ρ = 0.5 and deposit Q / L (Q = 2: 1 per ant on the short path, 0.5 per ant on the long path): short: 0.5 × 4 + 6 × 1 = 8; long: 0.5 × 1 + 2 × 0.5 = 1.5. Now P(short) = 8 / 9.5 = 0.84: the colony drifts to the shorter path.

4. Background: particle swarm optimisation (PSO)

  • Each particle is a candidate solution with a position x and a velocity v flying through the search space. It remembers its own best position (pbest) and knows the best position found by the swarm (gbest), like birds heading for where the flock found food.
  • Velocity update: vnew = w · v + c1 r1 (pbest − x) + c2 r2 (gbest − x); position update: xnew = x + vnew. Here w is inertia (keep going), c1 is the pull to its own best (memory), c2 the pull to the swarm's best (social), r1, r2 random numbers in (0, 1).

Worked example (illustrative): minimise f(x) = x². A particle at x = 4 (f = 16) with v = 1, pbest = 3, swarm best gbest = 1; w = 0.5, c1 = c2 = 1.5, r1 = 0.4, r2 = 0.6. vnew = 0.5 × 1 + 1.5 × 0.4 × (3 − 4) + 1.5 × 0.6 × (1 − 4) = 0.5 − 0.6 − 2.7 = −2.8. xnew = 4 + (−2.8) = 1.2, so f falls from 16 to 1.44. Repeat for every particle until the swarm converges; the answer is gbest.

Written questions

W1. What does your course say about swarm intelligence, and where does it sit among AI techniques?

Swarm intelligence (SI) is behaviour inspired by ants, birds and similar groups. It is one of the classical and complementary AI techniques, alongside genetic algorithms (inspired by evolution), fuzzy logic (degrees of truth) and rule-based systems, and beside machine learning. These methods can be hybridised (e.g. learning classifier systems combine GA and ML). Particle swarm and ant colony optimisation also appear on the GA slides' meta-heuristic map. Your folder contains nothing more on SI.

W2. (Background) Compare swarm intelligence and genetic algorithms.

Both are nature-inspired, population-based meta-heuristics that need only objective values, not derivatives. A GA evolves chromosomes by selection, crossover, mutation and replacement; in swarm methods the agents (ants, particles) persist and are guided by their own experience and that of neighbours (pheromone trails, pbest and gbest).

W3. (Background) Explain ant colony optimisation, including pheromone, positive feedback and evaporation.

Ants deposit pheromone as they walk and prefer stronger trails. A shorter path is completed faster, so it collects more pheromone (positive feedback) and more ants choose it; evaporation (τ ← (1 − ρ)τ + deposits) fades poor paths. Example: pheromone 4 vs 1 gives P(short) = 0.8; after 6 ants deposit 1 each on the short path and 2 deposit 0.5 on the long one with ρ = 0.5, the values are 8 and 1.5, so P(short) = 0.84.
