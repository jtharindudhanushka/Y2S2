  • Explain the role of transducers and classify sensors (digital, analog, both) and actuators

  • Design a transistor / MOSFET / relay driver and explain the flyback diode

1. What is a transducer?

  • Converts energy or physical quantities from one form to another.
  • Used at the boundary of an embedded system.
  • Classified by the direction of conversion: sensors (physical quantity → electrical signal, inputs) and actuators (electrical energy → other forms, outputs).

2. Sensors

Sensors capture the state of the physical world as inputs. They are digital (two discrete voltages, read through a digital input pin with PINx / digitalRead), analog (a continuous voltage, read with the ADC) or both.

3. Actuators

Actuators convert electrical energy into other forms and change the state of the physical world. Many need a higher voltage or more current than an I/O pin can deliver.

The ATmega328P pin limits: about 20 mA per pin recommended (40 mA absolute maximum), about 200 mA total for the chip. A small DC motor, a relay coil, a solenoid or a bulb will exceed this, so a driver is needed.

4. Driving a load with a BJT (BC547)

  • An NPN BJT is a current-controlled switch: a small base current lets a much larger current flow from collector to emitter. (PNP is the complementary type.)
  • Circuit: microcontroller pin → base resistor RB → base; emitter to GND (common with the microcontroller); load between the load supply +V and the collector.
  • Flyback diode across an inductive load (motor, relay, solenoid): cathode to +V, anode to the collector. When the transistor switches off, the coil's collapsing field drives current back through the diode instead of producing a high voltage spike that could destroy the transistor or the pin. The kit uses a 1N4001 rectifier diode (50 V reverse, 1 A forward, about 1.1 V forward drop).

Choosing RB

To saturate the transistor (fully on) force the base current to about one tenth of the collector current: IB ≈ IC / 10 (less than the gain hFE, which is 110 or more for a BC547). Then RB = (Vpin − VBE) / IB with Vpin = 5 V and VBE ≈ 0.7 V.

Worked exampleA 6 V motor draws 100 mA. IB = 100 mA / 10 = 10 mA. RB = (5 − 0.7) / 0.010 = 430 Ω, use 330 Ω or 470 Ω (the pin supplies 13 mA at 330 Ω, still within the pin limit). With 1 kΩ the base current is 4.3 mA, which saturates the transistor only up to about 43 mA. The BC547 handles up to 100 mA continuous (45 V VCEO); above that choose a power transistor.

5. Driving a load with a MOSFET (2N7000)

  • An N-channel enhancement MOSFET is voltage controlled: the gate draws practically no current in steady state. Terminals: gate G, drain D (load side), source S (to GND).
  • 2N7000: 60 V drain-source, 200 mA continuous, gate threshold 0.8 to 3 V, so a 5 V pin turns it on fully (logic-level). On-resistance a few ohms at VGS = 4.5 to 10 V.
  • Wiring: pin → gate (optionally through about 100 Ω to limit switching current); a 10 kΩ pull-down from gate to GND keeps it off while the pin floats (during reset or programming); load from +V to drain; source to GND; flyback diode across inductive loads.

6. Switching with a relay

  • A relay is an electromagnetic switch. A small coil current (from a transistor driver) closes the contacts of a separate, isolated circuit, so the microcontroller never touches the load voltage.
  • Contacts: COM (common), NO (normally open), NC (normally closed). The coil needs a transistor driver and a flyback diode. Relay modules include the transistor, diode and an indicator LED.
  • Use a relay (or a solid-state relay) for AC mains loads. Only with qualified supervision, correct isolation and enclosure.

7. Reading a digital sensor (code pattern)

// Hall switch AH1815 on PD2 (output low when a magnet is near), motor driver on PB0
DDRB |=  (1<<DDB0);                  // motor transistor base
DDRD &= ~(1<<DDD2);                  // sensor input
PORTD |= (1<<PORTD2);                 // pull-up for the open-drain output
while (1) {
    if ((PIND & (1<<PIND2)) == 0)     // magnet present
        PORTB |=  (1<<PORTB0);        // motor on
    else
        PORTB &= ~(1<<PORTB0);        // motor off
}
// Arduino
void setup() { pinMode(8, OUTPUT); pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP); }
void loop()  { digitalWrite(8, digitalRead(2) == LOW); }

8. Diode applications (reference reading)

  • Rectification (1N400X series): AC to DC; 1N4001 withstands 50 V reverse and 1 A.
  • Flyback / freewheeling protection across relay coils, motors and solenoids.
  • Reverse-polarity protection in series with a battery input.
  • LED indicators (light emitting diodes), signal clamping and logic.
Common mistakes1) Driving a motor straight from a pin. 2) Forgetting the flyback diode or fitting it backwards. 3) Not joining the grounds of the motor supply and the Arduino. 4) Using no pull-down on a MOSFET gate. 5) RB too large to saturate the BJT, which makes it hot. 6) Powering a motor from the 5 V pin of the Arduino (use a separate supply). 7) Connecting a mains circuit on a breadboard.
